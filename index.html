INCIDENT RESEARCH GUIDE

UNDERSTANDING AVIATION CRASHES

An aviation accident investigation usually reconstructs a sequence of events using multiple categories of evidence. The exact evidence available varies by case.

Aircraft & Systems

Aircraft type, configuration, maintenance history, engines, flight controls, electrical systems, navigation equipment, and warnings may be examined.

Flight Sequence

Investigators can reconstruct departure, altitude, speed, routing, communications, deviations, emergency actions, and the final known flight path.

Weather & Environment

Visibility, wind, precipitation, icing, turbulence, terrain, runway conditions, and other environmental factors may be relevant.

Human & Operational Factors

Crew workload, procedures, training, communication, air traffic control, dispatch, and operational conditions can be investigated.

Evidence Recovery

Flight recorders, cockpit voice recordings, wreckage, photographs, radar information, communications, maintenance records, and witness information can contribute to a reconstruction.

Findings

Investigators may identify causes and contributing factors, then issue findings and safety recommendations based on the available evidence.
