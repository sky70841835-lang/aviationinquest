INCIDENT RESEARCH GUIDE

UNDERSTANDING AVIATION CRASHES

Aircraft & Systems

Investigations examine the aircraft type, configuration, maintenance history, engines, flight controls, electrical systems, navigation equipment, warnings, and other technical factors that may have influenced the event.

Flight Sequence

A crash investigation reconstructs the timeline of the flight, including departure, altitude, speed, route, communications, deviations, emergency actions, and the final known flight path.

Weather & Environment

Investigators may examine visibility, precipitation, wind, icing, turbulence, terrain, runway conditions, daylight, and other environmental conditions surrounding the incident.

Human & Operational Factors

Crew workload, decision-making, procedures, training, communication, air traffic control, dispatch, and operational conditions can all be relevant to understanding an accident.

Evidence Recovery

Accident investigations can incorporate flight recorders, cockpit voice recordings, aircraft wreckage, photographs, maintenance records, radar data, communications, and witness information.

Final Findings

The final conclusion of an investigation is based on the available evidence and may identify contributing factors, safety recommendations, and lessons intended to prevent similar accidents in the future.
